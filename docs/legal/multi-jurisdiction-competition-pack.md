# OpenStore Multi-Jurisdiction Competition and Regulatory Pack

This document expands the OpenStore legal-preparation packet beyond the U.S. and Korea. It is a practical filing-preparation map, not legal advice.

## Scope

This pack is designed for situations where Apple's review conduct, platform restrictions, or app-distribution policies may justify competition-law or digital-market escalation outside the United States.

It currently covers:

- Korea
- Japan
- European Union
- United Kingdom
- Canada
- New Zealand
- Australia
- Iceland
- China
- India
- United Arab Emirates
- Saudi Arabia
- Vietnam
- Thailand
- Indonesia
- Philippines
- Russia

## Global Use Rules

- Use `templates/legal-intake.yaml` and `templates/jurisdiction-tracker.csv` as the source of truth.
- Do not claim jurisdiction-specific harm without actual local nexus facts.
- Do not assume the same Apple facts, filing form, or language requirements across jurisdictions.
- Do not assert that Apple is already under a general duty worldwide to host third-party app marketplaces.
- Keep the competition theory focused on exclusionary, discriminatory, opaque, or selectively enforced conduct.
- Expect translation, notarisation, or local representative requirements in many non-English jurisdictions.

## Current OpenStore-Safe Global Theory

The safest cross-border theory, on the current repository record, is:

- OpenStore is a nascent competitive app-marketplace project.
- Apple controls critical distribution and review access points for iOS apps.
- If Apple uses opaque, shifting, selectively enforced, or pretextual review conduct to block OpenStore, that conduct may support competition-law scrutiny in jurisdictions where OpenStore has a real business nexus.

## Jurisdiction Notes

### Korea

- Main authority: Korea Fair Trade Commission.
- Official path re-checked on 2026-03-20: `https://case.ftc.go.kr/main.do`, `https://case.ftc.go.kr/ocp/co/rcenoMth.do`, `https://case.ftc.go.kr/ocp/co/caseProcss.do`
- Existing repository document: `openstore-kftc-complaint-pack.md`
- Current safe hooks:
  - Abuse of market dominance
  - Unfair trade practice
- Filing note:
  - Prepare a Korean-language complaint and exhibit set before online filing.

### Japan

- Main authority: Japan Fair Trade Commission.
- Official paths re-checked on 2026-03-20:
  - Antimonopoly Act complaint route: `https://www.jftc.go.jp/soudan/madoguchi/shinkoku.html`
  - Digital and IT information-sharing route: `https://www.jftc.go.jp/soudan/johoteikyo.html`
- Current safe hook:
  - Antimonopoly Act concerns tied to exclusionary conduct by a digital platform or dominant intermediary.
- Filing note:
  - If OpenStore has a Japan nexus, use the formal complaint route for a statutory complaint and the IT/digital route for preliminary information-sharing.
  - Japanese-language preparation is strongly advisable.

### European Union

- Main authority: European Commission DG Competition, plus national competition authorities where the issue is limited to one or a small number of Member States.
- Official paths re-checked on 2026-03-20:
  - Complaints page: `https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/antitrust-and-cartels/procedures/complaints_en`
  - DG Competition contact page: `https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/antitrust-and-cartels/contact_en`
  - Electronic submissions: `https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/index/electronic-submissions_en`
  - DMA sector page: `https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/digital-media-electronic-communications_en`
- Current safe hooks:
  - Articles 101 and 102 TFEU where conduct restricts competition
  - Digital Markets Act issues where Apple conduct intersects with EU gatekeeper obligations
- Filing note:
  - Formal complaints require a legitimate interest and Form C.
  - If the issue is limited to one to three Member States, an NCA may be the better first stop.
  - Translate exhibits into an EU procedural language if originals are in a non-EU language.

### United Kingdom

- Main authority: Competition and Markets Authority.
- Official paths re-checked on 2026-03-20:
  - Report a problem: `https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tell-the-cma-about-a-competition-or-market-problem`
  - Report anti-competitive activity: `https://www.gov.uk/cartels-price-fixing/report-anticompetitive-activity`
  - Digital markets regime guidance: `https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/digital-markets-competition-regime-guidance`
  - Regime overview: `https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-the-uks-digital-markets-competition-regime-works`
- Current safe hooks:
  - Competition Act 1998 style exclusionary conduct theories
  - Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 digital markets regime where relevant
- Filing note:
  - The UK digital markets regime is live as of 2025, but Apple-specific strategic market status outcomes should be checked before any filing theory is finalised.

### Canada

- Main authority: Competition Bureau Canada.
- Official paths re-checked on 2026-03-20:
  - Share information: `https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/compliance-and-enforcement/share-information-competition-bureau`
  - Complaint form: `https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/contact-competition-bureau-canada/complaint-form`
  - Contact page: `https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/contact-competition-bureau-canada`
  - Abuse of dominance bulletin: `https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/consultations/bulletin-amendments-abuse-dominance-provisions`
- Current safe hooks:
  - Abuse of dominance
  - Other anti-competitive conduct depending on facts
- Filing note:
  - The Bureau accepts reports online and by mail.
  - Canada's current materials indicate that private parties can now apply to the Competition Tribunal in certain abuse-of-dominance circumstances if directly and substantially affected.

### New Zealand

- Main authority: Commerce Commission.
- Official paths re-checked on 2026-03-20:
  - Report a concern: `https://www.comcom.govt.nz/report-a-concern`
  - Competitor complaint guidance: `https://www.comcom.govt.nz/business/dealing-with-typical-situations/when-a-consumer-or-competitor-raises-a-complaint`
- Current safe hooks:
  - Commerce Act theories tied to anti-competitive conduct by a firm with market power
- Filing note:
  - The Commission accepts concerns through an online form and phone channels.
  - Use New Zealand nexus facts, not broader APAC facts, in any complaint narrative.

### Australia

- Main authority: Australian Competition and Consumer Commission.
- Official paths re-checked on 2026-03-20:
  - Competition and anti-competitive behaviour: `https://www.accc.gov.au/business/competition-and-exemptions/competition-and-anti-competitive-behaviour`
  - Contact or report an issue: `https://www.accc.gov.au/contact-us`
  - Digital Platform Services Inquiry final report: `https://www.accc.gov.au/inquiries-and-consultations/finalised-inquiries/digital-platform-services-inquiry-2020-25/march-2025-final-report`
- Current safe hooks:
  - Competition and Consumer Act 2010 anti-competitive conduct
  - Digital-platform competition concerns, especially around app marketplaces and mobile operating systems
- Filing note:
  - The ACCC explicitly says anyone can report anti-competitive business behaviour.
  - Keep any Australia section tied to Australian users, developers, partners, or launch plans.

### Iceland

- Main authority: Icelandic Competition Authority.
- Official paths re-checked on 2026-03-20:
  - Inquiries and complaints: `https://en.samkeppni.is/competition-rules/inquiries-and-complaints/`
  - Requirements for complaints: `https://en.samkeppni.is/competition-rules/inquiries-and-complaints/requirements-for-complaints/`
  - Procedure: `https://en.samkeppni.is/competition_authority/procedure/`
- Current safe hooks:
  - Abuse of dominant position
  - Collusion or public obstacles to competition where supported by facts
- Filing note:
  - The authority distinguishes between a formal complaint and a tip.
  - Formal complaints have detailed content requirements, including evidence, protected-interest reasoning, and additional copies.
  - Because the authority's English complaint pages were not consistently script-accessible in this workflow, re-open and confirm the live filing pages on the filing date.

### China

- Main authority: State Administration for Market Regulation / National Anti-Monopoly Bureau.
- Official paths re-checked on 2026-03-20:
  - SAMR home: `https://www.samr.gov.cn/`
  - 2026 complaint-handling rules update: `https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2026/art_e4d03a20c0fd49769e408c7bf3791ff5.html`
  - SAMR interaction page referencing the national `12315` platform: `https://www.samr.gov.cn/hd/index.html`

- Filing note:
  - Current official materials indicate that suspected violations are routed through SAMR rules and published intake channels, including the national `12315` platform.
  - A Chinese-language filing strategy and local counsel are strongly recommended.

### India

- Main authority: Competition Commission of India.
- Official paths re-checked on 2026-03-20:
  - Filing page: `https://www.cci.gov.in/filing/atd`
  - Main site filing section: `https://www.cci.gov.in/`
- Current safe hooks:
  - Information under section 19(1)(a) for anti-competitive agreements or abuse of dominant position causing or likely to cause appreciable adverse effect on competition in India
- Filing note:
  - The official filing page currently states who can file, what can be filed, address and format requirements, and fee bands under the 2024 regulations.
  - India-specific market and harm facts should be clearly separated from global product rhetoric.
