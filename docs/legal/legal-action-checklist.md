# Legal Action Checklist for OpenStore

This checklist is a practical preparation list for disputes with Apple related to App Store access, review delay, discriminatory treatment, or exclusionary conduct. It is not legal advice.

## Objective

Build a record that supports one or more of the following:

- A formal notice and preservation demand to Apple
- A complaint to the Korea Fair Trade Commission
- Complaints or reports to U.S. competition authorities
- A request for emergency or preliminary injunctive relief
- A later damages or unfair competition action

## Phase 1: Standing and Entity Readiness

- Confirm which legal entity will submit the iOS app and send legal notices.
- Confirm the exact Apple Developer account holder name, address, and account identifier.
- Decide whether the same entity will also be the complainant before the Korea Fair Trade Commission.
- Confirm who owns the codebase, trademarks, product name, and submission rights.
- Prepare a clean corporate file with certificate of incorporation, business registration, registered address, and signer authority.
- Identify outside counsel in:
  - U.S. antitrust and platform litigation
  - Korean competition and administrative law
- Decide who can approve escalation within 24 hours if Apple rejects or stalls.

## Phase 2: Product and Submission Record

- Freeze the exact product theory:
  - What OpenStore does today
  - What it will do in version 1
  - What Apple is likely to say it does
- Prepare a one-page product description using precise language only.
- Prepare a one-page explanation of why the product is pro-competitive and user-benefiting.
- Maintain a submission log with:
  - Build number
  - Submission date and time
  - Reviewer messages
  - Resolution Center messages
  - Calls or meetings
  - Escalations
  - Response deadlines
- Capture screenshots and exports from App Store Connect at every meaningful step.
- Save all rejection notices verbatim.
- Keep a timeline of every Apple communication with exact dates.

## Phase 3: Evidence Preservation

- Put an internal litigation hold in place now for all OpenStore personnel involved in product, policy, Apple communications, and strategy.
- Preserve:
  - Slack, email, Notion, docs, meeting notes, and product specs
  - Submission packages and prior build artifacts
  - Internal analyses about Apple, market structure, and competitive impact
  - Financial models showing expected harm from exclusion
- Keep an external evidence folder with:
  - Apple guidelines cited against you
  - Apple policy changes over time
  - Examples of Apple allowing comparable or adjacent apps
  - Public statements by Apple about security, competition, or distribution
- Keep hashes or immutable copies of critical evidence where feasible.

## Phase 4: Apple-Facing Process

- Submit the app with deliberately careful metadata and screenshots.
- Avoid unnecessary marketing claims in the first review submission.
- Respond to Apple only in writing where possible.
- Ask Apple to identify:
  - The exact guideline or contract provision
  - The exact product behavior at issue
  - Whether the decision is categorical or fixable
- Escalate from ordinary review to formal review communication quickly if Apple becomes vague or repetitive.
- Send the formal notice letter once there is:
  - A rejection
  - A material delay
  - A pattern of shifting rationales
  - A demand that appears pretextual or discriminatory
- Keep Apple responses grouped by issue so later filings can show inconsistency.

## Phase 5: Harm Record

- Build a damages and harm memo that is updated continuously.
- Track:
  - Lost launch time
  - Lost users
  - Lost developer interest
  - Lost partnerships
  - Fundraising impact
  - Reputational harm
  - Additional engineering and compliance costs caused by Apple
- Keep third-party declarations or emails from developers, partners, or users who were affected by Apple-related delay.
- Document why delay itself harms competition, not just rejection.

## Phase 6: Korea Fair Trade Commission Preparation

- Decide the primary Korean theory:
  - Abuse of market dominance
  - Unfair trade practice
  - Both, if supported by facts
- Prepare a Korean filing package with:
  - Market definition summary
  - Apple's control points
  - Exclusionary conduct theory
  - Timeline of submission and rejection
  - Evidence of selective or pretextual enforcement
  - Harm to competition, developers, and users
- Translate the key U.S. materials into Korean:
  - Apple rejection notices
  - Apple guidelines at issue
  - The formal notice letter
  - Key product explanations
- Prepare a Korean chronology with exact dates and document citations.
- Be ready to file through the KFTC online case system.

Official references checked for this workflow:

- KFTC online case system main page: https://case.ftc.go.kr/main.do
- KFTC case filing method page: https://case.ftc.go.kr/ocp/co/rcenoMth.do
- KFTC process overview page: https://case.ftc.go.kr/ocp/co/caseProcss.do

Search snippets from KFTC's current site indicate:

- The online case system supports online filing and case progress lookup.
- The KFTC consultation line is `1670-0007`.

## Phase 7: U.S. Regulatory Preparation

- Prepare a U.S. regulator packet in English that can be sent with minimal editing.
- Keep a short regulator summary limited to:
  - Who Apple is
  - What OpenStore is
  - What Apple did
  - Why the conduct is exclusionary
  - Why urgency matters
- Prepare a longer annex with evidence citations.
- Be ready to submit a report to the U.S. Department of Justice Antitrust Division.
- Be ready to submit a complaint to the FTC Bureau of Competition.

Official references checked for this workflow:

- DOJ Antitrust complaint webform: https://www.justice.gov/atr/webform/submit-your-antitrust-report-online
- DOJ report violations page: https://www.justice.gov/atr/report-violations
- FTC antitrust complaint intake: https://www.ftc.gov/advice-guidance/competition-guidance/antitrust-complaint-intake
- FTC anticompetitive practices overview: https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/anticompetitive-practices
- FTC contact page: https://www.ftc.gov/about-ftc/contact

Current official U.S. guidance indicates:

- DOJ accepts online antitrust reports and allows anonymous reporting.
- DOJ says reports are more useful when they explain the conduct, affected parties, and harm to prices, quality, innovation, and customer choice.
- FTC directs antitrust complaints to its Bureau of Competition complaint intake form.

## Phase 8: Litigation Preparation

- Prepare for the possibility that the first serious relief sought is an injunction, not damages.
- Identify the most favorable forum candidates with U.S. counsel.
- Prepare declarations from:
  - Founder or CEO
  - Product lead
  - Technical lead
  - Affected developers or partners if available
- Prepare an exhibit set with clean numbering and source files.
- Prepare a witness map showing who can testify to:
  - Product design
  - Apple communications
  - Harm
  - Market facts
- Preserve engineering evidence showing what the app actually does and does not do.
- Avoid changing the product description casually after a dispute starts unless changes are tracked and explained.

## Phase 9: Public and Investor Communications

- Draft a neutral external statement for use if Apple rejects or delays the app.
- Keep public messaging aligned with the legal theory.
- Do not publicly overclaim that Apple is already legally required in the U.S. to host a third-party app marketplace.
- Do state, if supported by facts, that Apple used opaque or discriminatory review conduct to block a competitive product.
- Prepare a separate investor memo and a separate regulator memo. Do not reuse one for both without editing.

## Phase 10: Trigger-Based Escalation Rules

Escalate to formal legal action immediately if any of the following occur:

- Apple issues a categorical rejection tied to OpenStore's competitive nature.
- Apple gives shifting reasons across review rounds.
- Apple stops engaging on the actual text of its own guidelines.
- Apple demands product changes unrelated to a real safety, privacy, or technical issue.
- Apple delays review long enough to impair launch, financing, or partner commitments.
- Apple treats comparable products differently without a coherent explanation.

## Immediate Top-Priority Tasks

- Finalize the product description that will be used consistently in review, regulator packets, and court papers.
- Build the evidence folder and submission timeline before the first serious Apple dispute.
- Retain or shortlist U.S. and Korean competition counsel.
- Prepare the Korean complaint pack template and the U.S. regulator pack template now, not after rejection.
- Keep the Apple notice letter updated with real facts so it can be sent the same day a dispute becomes concrete.
