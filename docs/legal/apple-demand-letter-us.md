# Draft Notice Letter to Apple (U.S.-Focused)

This draft is written in English for delivery to Apple. It is a business and legal-preparation draft, not legal advice. Before sending, replace all bracketed placeholders and have U.S. antitrust counsel review the final version if possible.

## Recommended Use

Use this draft when:

- Apple has rejected, delayed, or threatened to reject `[App Name]`.
- Apple is relying on shifting, vague, or selectively enforced review rationales.
- You want to create a clear written record before escalating to regulators or seeking injunctive relief.

If you have not yet submitted the app, this draft can still be used as a pre-dispute notice and preservation demand, but it is stronger once tied to a concrete submission, rejection, or delay.

## Draft Letter

```text
[Sender Legal Name]
[Sender Address]
[Sender Email]
[Sender Phone]

[Date]

VIA INTERNATIONAL COURIER, EMAIL, AND APP STORE CONNECT

Apple Inc.
Attn: Legal Department / Apple Developer Relations
One Apple Park Way
Cupertino, California 95014
United States

Re: Notice of Objection to Exclusionary Review Conduct, Demand for Non-Discriminatory Review,
    and Demand to Preserve Evidence Concerning [App Name] / Bundle ID [Bundle ID]

To Apple Legal and Apple Developer Relations:

We write regarding [App Name], a software product developed by [Company Name] for distribution to
users on iOS and/or related Apple platforms. [Company Name] is preparing to submit, or has submitted,
[App Name] through Apple's developer and App Store review process under Apple Developer account
[Account Identifier].

This letter provides formal notice that [Company Name] objects to any exclusionary, discriminatory,
pretextual, or bad-faith use of Apple's review process, contractual discretion, technical restrictions,
or policy enforcement to delay, burden, or deny the distribution of [App Name] because the product
competes, directly or indirectly, with Apple's App Store or app-distribution business.

To be clear, [Company Name] is not demanding that Apple waive legitimate, neutral, consistently
applied security, privacy, safety, or platform-integrity requirements. We recognize that Apple
currently maintains platform rules that it may cite, including technical and review restrictions on
apps that download, install, or execute additional code, and Apple currently provides certain
alternative distribution pathways only in the European Union. Our objection is narrower and more
serious: Apple may not lawfully weaponize discretionary review, selectively enforced guidelines,
shifting standards, or pretextual "integrity" rationales to suppress an incipient competitive threat.

If Apple rejects, delays, burdens, or conditions approval of [App Name] in a manner that is not
objectively necessary, consistently applied, and supportable by the actual text and historical
application of Apple's policies, [Company Name] will view that conduct as potential exclusionary
conduct and unfair competition under applicable law, including but not limited to Section 2 of the
Sherman Act, Section 16 of the Clayton Act, and California's Unfair Competition Law, in addition to
any other claims and remedies available under contract, tort, competition, or foreign law.

Relevant facts include:

1. [Company Name] developed [App Name], which [brief, accurate product description].
2. [App Name] [was submitted / will be submitted] on [date] under submission/build number [number].
3. Apple [rejected / delayed / requested repeated changes / failed to provide a clear position] on
   [date(s)].
4. Apple cited the following rationale(s): [quote or summarize actual rejection text].
5. Apple has not provided a clear, objective, consistently applicable explanation of why the cited
   rationale requires rejection, delay, or additional burdens in this instance.
6. [If true] The timing, wording, and substance of Apple's conduct reasonably suggest concern with
   competitive impact rather than any genuine, proportionate, and consistently applied review issue.

Accordingly, [Company Name] demands that Apple:

1. Conduct and complete review of [App Name] promptly, fairly, and in good faith.
2. Identify in writing each specific contractual provision, guideline, technical rule, or policy on
   which Apple relies for any actual or threatened rejection, delay, restriction, or condition.
3. State in writing the precise factual basis for each position, including what exact product behavior
   Apple contends violates which exact rule.
4. Confirm whether the same interpretation and enforcement position has been applied consistently to
   similarly situated apps, products, or services, including Apple's own offerings where relevant.
5. Confirm that Apple is not taking competitive impact into account, directly or indirectly, in
   deciding whether and on what terms [App Name] may be distributed.
6. Provide a clear, single, technically competent point of contact for further discussion and
   escalation.

In addition, [Company Name] hereby demands that Apple preserve all documents and information
potentially relevant to the review, treatment, competitive evaluation, or internal discussion of
[App Name], [Company Name], and any competing or potentially competing app-distribution model.
This preservation demand includes, without limitation:

- App Review records, reviewer notes, escalation records, and internal decision logs;
- Internal emails, chats, messages, calendar invites, meeting notes, presentations, and memoranda;
- Communications among App Review, Developer Relations, Legal, Policy, Finance, Product, Strategy,
  Trust & Safety, and executive personnel;
- Analyses concerning competitive effects, cannibalization, commissions, App Store revenue, market
  impact, platform-control strategy, steering, sideloading, marketplace competition, or alternative
  distribution;
- Communications referencing [App Name], [Company Name], [App category], or comparable products;
- Prior enforcement materials showing how Apple has interpreted or applied the same rules to other
  developers.

Apple should also suspend any ordinary-document-destruction, log-deletion, ephemeral-messaging, or
auto-delete process that could affect relevant evidence.

Please provide a written response no later than ten (10) business days from receipt of this letter
confirming:

1. Whether Apple will permit prompt, good-faith review of [App Name];
2. Whether Apple contends that [App Name] is categorically ineligible for distribution and, if so,
   the exact legal and policy basis for that position;
3. Whether Apple has implemented a litigation hold and preservation measures responsive to this
   notice; and
4. The name, title, and contact details of the Apple representative authorized to address this matter.

If Apple proceeds with rejection, delay, or discriminatory conditions without a clear and legitimate
basis, [Company Name] will consider immediate further action, including requests for injunctive
relief, complaints to competition and consumer-protection authorities, and any other available legal
remedies, without further notice.

Nothing in this letter waives any right, claim, remedy, argument, or position, all of which are
expressly reserved.

Sincerely,

[Name]
[Title]
[Company Name]
```

## Practical Notes Before Sending

- Replace placeholders with provable facts only. Do not overstate what Apple said or did.
- Attach the actual rejection notices, Resolution Center messages, submission history, and screenshots.
- If Apple has not acted yet, change the facts section so it says the company reasonably anticipates discriminatory review based on Apple's rules and market position.
- Send the same PDF by courier and through the App Store Connect communication channel so Apple cannot later say the issue was never raised through the normal review record.
- Keep the tone factual. The goal is to build a clean evidentiary record for later injunctive or regulatory action.

## Why This Draft Is Framed This Way

- It does not falsely claim that Apple is already under a general U.S. duty to allow third-party app marketplaces in the United States.
- It does preserve the stronger argument that Apple cannot use discretionary review and selectively enforced restrictions to suppress a competitive threat.
- It asks for evidence preservation early, which matters if you later need to show pretext, inconsistent enforcement, or competition-related motive.

## Reference Basis

These materials informed the framing of this draft:

- Apple App Review Guidelines: Apple states that apps must be self-contained and may not download, install, or execute code that changes features or functionality, which is the policy Apple is likely to cite against any marketplace-like product in the U.S.  
  https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/
- Apple EU-only alternative distribution materials: Apple expressly describes Web Distribution and alternative marketplace pathways as EU-specific.  
  https://developer.apple.com/support/web-distribution-eu/
- Apple Developer Program License Agreement: Apple states it is under no obligation to display, feature, or rank an app in any particular manner, which is why the letter focuses on discriminatory or exclusionary enforcement rather than discoverability rights.  
  https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/
- Epic v. Apple, Ninth Circuit (2023): the Ninth Circuit affirmed injunctive relief tied to Apple's anti-steering restrictions under California's UCL and recognized competitive harm to a rival distributor.  
  https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/07/17/21-16506.pdf
- Epic v. Apple, Ninth Circuit (2025): the Ninth Circuit later affirmed core contempt findings and left in place relief addressing Apple's failure to comply with the injunction.  
  https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2025/12/11/25-2935.pdf
- Epic v. Apple, N.D. Cal. (Apr. 30, 2025): the district court found Apple had continued anticompetitive conduct and immediately enjoined specific practices that interfered with competition.  
  https://cdn.arstechnica.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Contempt-order.pdf
- U.S. antitrust statutes often used in this context: Sherman Act Section 2, Clayton Act Sections 4 and 16.  
  https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2  
  https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/15  
  https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/26
