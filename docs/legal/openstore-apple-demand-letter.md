# OpenStore Apple Demand Letter Package

This document converts the generic Apple notice concept into an OpenStore-specific working draft. It is not legal advice. Before sending, replace all placeholders with provable facts and have U.S. counsel review the final text if available.

## When To Use This Draft

Use this draft when one or more of the following is true:

- Apple rejects an OpenStore-related submission
- Apple materially delays review and will not give a clear position
- Apple gives shifting or vague review rationales
- Apple demands product changes that appear unrelated to a genuine technical, safety, or privacy concern
- OpenStore wants to create an evidence-preservation record before a likely dispute becomes concrete

## OpenStore Facts Already Fixed

These product facts are already supported by the repository and can be used without further invention:

- OpenStore is an open source alternative to the Apple App Store.
- The repository currently contains an initial storefront prototype.
- The project direction is to become a fuller marketplace product over time.
- The repository does not, by itself, prove a current App Store submission, review, rejection, or build identifier.

## Working Draft

```text
[Sender Legal Name]
[Sender Address]
[Sender Email]
[Sender Phone]

[Date]

VIA INTERNATIONAL COURIER, EMAIL, AND APP STORE CONNECT

Apple Inc.
Attn: Legal Department / Apple Developer Relations
One Apple Park Way
Cupertino, California 95014
United States

Re: OpenStore / Bundle ID [Bundle ID]
    Notice of Objection, Demand for Good-Faith Review, and Preservation Demand

To Apple Legal and Apple Developer Relations:

We write regarding OpenStore, a software product developed by [Company Name]. OpenStore is an
open source alternative app marketplace project. The current product record maintained by the
company describes OpenStore as a web-first storefront prototype that includes catalog discovery,
app detail, search, library, account, review, and reporting surfaces, while the broader product
direction is to evolve toward a fuller marketplace offering over time.

[Company Name] is preparing to submit, or has submitted, OpenStore through Apple's developer and
App Store review process under Apple Developer account [Apple Developer Account Holder and
Identifier]. This letter provides formal notice that [Company Name] objects to any exclusionary,
discriminatory, pretextual, or bad-faith use of Apple's review process, contractual discretion,
technical restrictions, or policy enforcement to delay, burden, or deny distribution of OpenStore
because the product competes, directly or indirectly, with Apple's App Store or app-distribution
business.

To be clear, [Company Name] is not demanding that Apple waive legitimate, neutral, consistently
applied security, privacy, safety, or platform-integrity requirements. Our objection is narrower:
Apple should not use vague, shifting, selectively enforced, or pretextual review positions to
suppress an incipient competitive product.

The present record is as follows:

1. [Company Name] developed OpenStore as an open source alternative app marketplace project.
2. [If pre-submission] [Company Name] reasonably anticipates that Apple may treat OpenStore as a
   competitive threat and therefore seeks a clear, non-pretextual review record from the outset.
3. [If post-submission] OpenStore was submitted on [Submission Date] under build number
   [Build Number].
4. [If post-submission] Apple responded on [Apple Message Dates] with the following rationale(s):
   [Quoted Apple Review Language].
5. Apple has [not yet provided / not provided] a clear, objective, and consistently applicable
   explanation of why the identified product behavior requires [rejection / delay / additional
   conditions].

Accordingly, [Company Name] demands that Apple:

1. Conduct and complete review of OpenStore promptly, fairly, and in good faith.
2. Identify in writing each specific guideline, contract provision, technical rule, or policy on
   which Apple relies for any actual or threatened rejection, delay, restriction, or condition.
3. Identify the precise factual basis for each position, including the exact product behavior Apple
   contends violates the cited rule.
4. Confirm whether Apple is applying the same interpretation consistently to similarly situated
   products.
5. Provide a single technically competent point of contact for further escalation.

In addition, [Company Name] demands that Apple preserve all documents and information potentially
relevant to the review, treatment, and internal discussion of OpenStore, [Company Name], and any
competing or potentially competing app-distribution model, including:

- App Review records, reviewer notes, escalation records, and decision logs;
- Internal communications among App Review, Developer Relations, Legal, Policy, Finance, Product,
  Strategy, Trust & Safety, and executive personnel;
- Analyses concerning app-distribution competition, marketplace competition, alternative
  distribution, platform-control strategy, cannibalization, steering, or App Store revenue impact;
- Communications referencing OpenStore, [Company Name], its product category, or comparable
  products;
- Prior enforcement materials showing how Apple has interpreted or applied the same rules to other
  developers.

Apple should suspend ordinary document-destruction or auto-delete practices that could affect
relevant evidence.

Please provide a written response no later than ten (10) business days from receipt of this
letter confirming:

1. Whether Apple will permit prompt, good-faith review of OpenStore;
2. Whether Apple contends that OpenStore is categorically ineligible for distribution and, if so,
   the exact policy and factual basis for that position;
3. Whether Apple has implemented preservation measures responsive to this notice; and
4. The name, title, and contact details of the Apple representative authorized to address this
   matter.

Nothing in this letter waives any right, claim, remedy, argument, or position, all of which are
expressly reserved.

Sincerely,

[Signer Name]
[Signer Title]
[Company Name]
```

## Official Sources Re-Checked on 2026-03-20

- Apple App Review Guidelines section 2.5.2 says apps should be self-contained in their bundles and may not download, install, or execute code that introduces or changes app features or functionality: <https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/>
- Apple says Web Distribution is available to authorized developers for users in the European Union and describes EU-specific eligibility and requirements: <https://developer.apple.com/support/web-distribution-eu/>
- Apple Developer Program License Agreement says Apple is under no obligation to display, feature, or rank an app in any particular manner or order in the App Store, which is why the letter focuses on exclusionary or discriminatory enforcement rather than discoverability rights: <https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/>

## Practical Rule

This letter should stay narrowly factual. Do not claim that Apple is already under a general U.S. duty to carry third-party app marketplaces. The stronger record is usually that Apple cannot use opaque or selectively enforced review conduct to block a competitive product.
